Recommendation
Sound core, major revisions before claiming systematic-review compliance

The reproducibility machinery is stronger than most review tools, and anchoring every concept to real DOIs is the right defence against invented terms. The main scientific risk is that the anchor rule protects precision at the cost of recall, which a systematic search is judged on first. For end users, the barriers are jargon and rigidity rather than missing features.

Strengths

  • Reproducibility. Deterministic protocol compile, content-addressed recon cache, a protocol fingerprint and an append-only audit journal with a GENESIS event and a full recon_context.json sidecar.
  • Evidence before concepts. A direction needs two or more anchor DOIs and every core concept at least one. Nothing unanchored reaches core_concepts.
  • Careful signals. Corpus saturation is framed as a thinness signal, never a gap certificate, and the skill warns that the prompt-echo index reads backwards on well-focused pools.
  • Teaching value. The interview makes students state a paradigm, a unit of analysis, the proof a reviewer would accept, and what is out of scope.
  • One engine, two front ends. The chat agent imports the wizard's own functions, so both propose identical directions.

Major comments

M1

Unanchored synonyms are dropped, which hurts recall

Major

A synonym with no anchor in a pool of at most 25 papers is removed (grounded.py:477). Controlled vocabulary such as MeSH headings, regional spellings and older names will often be absent from a small, query-shaped sample.

RemedyCheck synonyms against the whole-corpus count (ReconEngine.corpus_count already exists), or keep them with a recorded expert rationale.

M2

Directions are frequent phrases, and their evidence is thin and circular

Major

Two DOIs out of a 25-paper pool drawn from one query is a low bar, and the pool reflects the wording of the topic. Directions are n-grams ranked multi-word first, then by frequency (grounded.py:153). They are lexical patterns, not research gaps or questions.

RemedyRename them in the interface (for example "recurring themes"), and consider citation or recency weighting, or semantic clustering.

M3

Gate thresholds come from a single trial

Major

The topic-purity threshold (0.50) and the thin-pool floor (12 papers) were calibrated on one multi-domain trial dated 13 September 2026 (gates.py).

RemedyLabel them provisional in the methods text and validate them across more fields before they appear in a publication.

M4

Semantic search results drift over time

Major

OpenAlex semantic results can change as the service updates. The cache keeps a project stable, but a later rerun by a reader may differ without explanation.

RemedyRecord the provider, retrieval date and search mode in the protocol itself, not only in the cache key.

M5

Systematic-review basics are missing at this stage

Major

Reviewers and journals expect: searches across several bibliographic databases (Scopus, Web of Science, PubMed as relevant), peer review of the search strategy against the PRESS checklist, piloting the criteria on a sample, dual screening, and protocol registration (PROSPERO or OSF).

RemedyAdd database selection and a registration export to inception, and a second-reviewer path to screening.

M6

The paradigm model is narrow

Moderate

Four paradigms detected from keywords (Positivist, Interpretivist, Design Science, Pragmatist / Mixed Methods) leave out critical realism, constructivist grounded theory and transformative approaches. Health and social-science reviewers will look first for PICO, SPIDER or PEO question frameworks.

RemedyOffer a question framework as an alternative entry to the paradigm cards, and make lexicon warnings advisory.

M7

Three proposed directions can steer the researcher

Moderate

Even with the human as final gate, a short machine-ranked list narrows thinking (automation bias).

RemedyShow the other anchored terms in the pool, and accept a researcher-defined direction that is anchored afterwards.

By user group

PhD students

Works
Structured thinking, a compiled protocol and an audit trail a committee can inspect. A dissertation playbook exists.
Hurts
Internal terms (QEI, purity, DESIGN_SCIENCE), the CLI, and hard exits that lose every answer.
Needs
Plain-language labels, save and resume, a graphical front end.

Supervisors

Works
Provenance: they can see why a concept is in the search, with the papers behind it.
Hurts
No way to comment on or approve a draft protocol.
Needs
A review loop, registration export, second-reviewer support.

Experienced researchers

Works
Headless flags for scripted runs; a deterministic, cached engine.
Hurts
A long guided interview for a field they know; machine-picked directions.
Needs
Import an existing protocol or seed papers, jump to any step, override an anchor rule with a reason.

Recommended order of work

  1. Protect recall. Whole-corpus synonym checks, expert overrides with a recorded reason, researcher-supplied seed papers.
  2. Translate the interface. Plain labels for users, raw metrics kept in an expandable detail line.
  3. Add question frameworks. PICO and SPIDER beside the paradigm cards.
  4. Strengthen the record. Provider, date and mode in the protocol; PROSPERO or OSF export.
  5. Support collaboration. A supervisor review loop and second-reviewer support before claiming systematic-review compliance.
