## The five wording checks
They join `02-checks` (inception, step `criteria`) in its format. Level, rule and constants are M11b (f); the sentence, the why and the action are proposed here for the researcher's read. The catalogue's own file is not edited by this draft; its table gains these rows when this spec is FINAL (note (b) under Open questions). All five read the **accepted** criteria of the saved draft (Open question 4). Words are normalised as in M11b (f): lower case, punctuation and stop words removed. Constants are engine values returned in `threshold`; none is editable.

| Check | Level | What it tests (M11b (f)) | Sentence | Why | Action |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| `CRITERIA_DUPLICATE` | warning, subject = the two ids | Two accepted criteria on the same side with equal normalised texts | "Two {side} criteria say the same thing: “{a}” and “{b}”." | "Screening reads each criterion on its own. The same test twice adds a line to read, and a record that fails it is counted under one of two ids." | Field `extra_inclusions` or `extra_exclusions`, focus the first row: "Look at these two" |
| `CRITERIA_CONTRADICT` | warning, subject = the two ids | An inclusion and an exclusion with equal normalised texts | "“{inclusion}” is an inclusion and “{exclusion}” is an exclusion with the same words." | "No record can be included: one that meets the inclusion fails the exclusion, and one that does not meet it fails the inclusion." | Field `extra_exclusions`, focus the exclusion row: "Look at these two" |
| `CRITERIA_MIRRORED` | warning, subject = the two ids | An exclusion whose content words, after the negation words (not, no, without, only, other than, non, except, excluding) are removed, cover at least 0.6 of an inclusion's content words, or the reverse | "The exclusion “{exclusion}” mostly repeats the inclusion “{inclusion}” with a negation." | "A record that fails the inclusion is then also caught by the exclusion, so one judgement counts twice, and an agent's draft answers both the same way. This check compares words, not meaning. Two criteria that say the same thing in different words are not caught." | Field `extra_exclusions`, focus the exclusion row: "Look at these two" |
| `CRITERIA_LUMPED` | note, subject = the id | A criterion with a semicolon, or a list of three or more items joined by commas and "or" or "and" | "“{criterion}” may be several tests in one." | "A record that meets part of it gets one answer for the whole line, and you and the agent may read it differently. One test per criterion is easier to judge and easier to revise after a pilot." | Field of its side, focus the row: "Look at this criterion" |
| `CRITERIA_NEEDS_FULL_TEXT` | note, subject = the id | A criterion naming something an abstract rarely reports, from a fixed word list (full text, results section, table, sample size, code available, data available, statistical test, reports the …) | "“{criterion}” may need the full text: it names {words}." | "Screening reads titles and abstracts. A criterion about something they rarely report will be answered “not reported” for many records, and the choice below decides what happens to them." | Field `method_not_reported` (this block's second question): "Choose what to do when it is not reported" |

What these rules cannot do, said once under the lists and again in the mirror check's why. The wording checks find equal words, not equal meaning. "RGB only" against "multispectral imagery from a UAV" share no word, so `CRITERIA_MIRRORED` does not catch the pair the 2026-11-20 review actually had; "uses a learned model" beside "or a dataset paper" is a contradiction of meaning that no word rule sees. Both are found only after a pilot, by the pilot's rules on the agent's answers when the researcher runs the agent check (`13-criteria-pilot`; the pilot's checklist stays closed, answer 6), or by the researcher's own AI client through the M10c critic. A clean list therefore means "no repeated words", never "the criteria are sound".

Each check is dismissed as `02-checks` says: a warning with a reason (free text, kept in the record), a note with no reason. A dismissal belongs to one `evidence_digest` (the texts it quotes), so editing either criterion reopens it with "You dismissed this on {date}, but the data changed since."

## The blocker `METHOD_RULE_UNCHOSEN`
A new blocker in the M10b catalogue (step `criteria`), computed by the engine from the saved draft, at the field (this block) and at Review, like `CRITERIA_UNDECIDED` (M11b (g), answer 8). Level `blocker`: not dismissible (`CHECK_NOT_DISMISSIBLE`), no Dismiss button. It is raised when `method_not_reported` is null, whatever the reason: a new draft, a draft started before part D, an import with no `method` block. One choice among the three clears it. It disables passing step 5 and genesis is refused through the existing blocker path naming `METHOD_RULE_UNCHOSEN`. Like every check it describes the saved draft, so a choice made in the tab clears it after the next Save (Open question 8 of this spec; the radio itself shows the choice at once).

| Case | Sentence | Why | Action |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| New draft, or an import with no `method` block | "Choose what the agent should do when a criterion is not reported." | "The title and abstract often do not report a criterion. The answer decides whether such a record is marked Unclear or excluded, and it is signed into the protocol. There is no default: this one is yours." | Field `method_not_reported`, focus the first radio: "Choose a rule" |
| Draft started before part D | The sentence above, then: "This draft was started before this question existed." | as above | as above |

The words of each case are keys (see "Copy"), in en, fr and ar (fr and ar fall back to en until translated, plan §7), in the style of the M10b checks (`checks.<ID>.sentence|why|action`).
